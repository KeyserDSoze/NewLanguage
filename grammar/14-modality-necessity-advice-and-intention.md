# 14 — Modality, Necessity, Advice, and Intention

STRING expresses modality with ordinary invariant words before the lexical verb.

No modal changes for person or number, and no following verb receives an infinitive marker.

## 1. Basic modal chain — CONFIRMED

The basic pattern is:

```text
SUBJECT + [TENSE] + MODAL + VERB
```

The lexical verb remains unchanged.

## 2. Ability — CONFIRMED

`kan` expresses ability and ordinary practical possibility.

```text
mi kan go.
I can go.

de did kan go.
They could go / were able to go.
```

Past ability uses ordinary `did`; STRING does not create a separate form equivalent to English `could`.

## 3. Possibility or permission — WORKING STANDARD

`me` expresses possibility and, where context makes the social meaning clear, permission.

```text
da me go.
He / she / it may go.

me mi go?
May I go?
```

STRING does not require separate inflected forms for English `may` and `might`.

A hypothetical context can combine ordinary STRING markers when necessary.

## 4. Need — CONFIRMED

`nid` expresses need.

```text
mi nid go.
I need to go.
```

There is no mandatory equivalent of English infinitive `to`.

## 5. Strong necessity or obligation — WORKING STANDARD

`mas` expresses strong necessity or obligation: **must**.

```text
yu mas go.
You must go.
```

`mas` is a reviewed shortening of the mechanically repaired English source. It is intentionally shorter than `masat` while remaining legal STRING.

## 6. Advice or expected action — WORKING STANDARD

`xud` expresses advice, recommendation, or an action judged appropriate: **should**.

```text
yu xud go.
You should go.
```

The form follows the English pronunciation closely: English /ʃʊd/ maps directly to STRING `xud`.

## 7. Desire or intention — WORKING STANDARD

`won` means **want** and can introduce another verb directly.

```text
mi won go.
I want to go.
```

A separate infinitive particle is unnecessary.

## 8. Tense with modality — CONFIRMED

Tense precedes the modal or dependency verb.

```text
mi did nid go.
I needed to go.

mi wil kan go.
I will be able to go.

mi did won go.
I wanted to go.
```

The modal and lexical verb remain invariant.

## 9. Negation and scope — WORKING STANDARD

`not` normally negates the next predicate operator.

Compare:

```text
mi not kan go.
I cannot go.
```

with:

```text
mi kan not go.
I can refrain from going / I am able not to go.
```

With explicit tense:

```text
mi did not kan go.
I was not able to go.

mi did kan not go.
I was able not to go.
```

This scope rule avoids creating special negative modal words.

## 10. Modal questions — CONFIRMED

The first predicate word moves before the subject, exactly as in other questions.

```text
kan yu go?
Can you go?

me mi go?
May I go?

mas yu go?
Must you go?

xud mi go?
Should I go?

won yu go?
Do you want to go?
```

No extra English-style `do` is inserted.

## 11. Multiple modal meanings — PROVISIONAL

STRING should avoid long stacks of grammatical modal words.

When more than one modal meaning is essential, prefer a transparent ordinary clause or lexical expression rather than creating a fixed hierarchy of many auxiliary classes.

The language should preserve clarity before compactness.
