# 09 — Commands and Complex Sentences

STRING builds complex sentences by combining ordinary clauses. It does not introduce special verb conjugations for commands, subordinate clauses, relative clauses, or conditions.

## 1. Commands — CONFIRMED

A direct command uses a normal predicate without an explicit subject.

```text
go!
Go!

bi gud!
Be good!
```

The omitted subject is the addressee.

The verb remains exactly the same dictionary form used in statements.

## 2. Negative commands — CONFIRMED

Place `not` before the predicate.

```text
not go!
Do not go!
```

No special negative imperative form exists.

## 3. Politeness — WORKING STANDARD

`piliz` means **please** and can soften a request or command.

```text
piliz go.
Please go.
```

Politeness does not change verb grammar.

## 4. Content clauses — WORKING STANDARD

`dat` can introduce a complete clause used as the content of another predicate.

```text
SUBJECT + PREDICATE + dat + CLAUSE
```

Example:

```text
mi si dat yu go.
I see that you go.
```

This use is related to English `that`. STRING uses the same accepted word rather than creating a separate complementizer.

## 5. Relative clauses — WORKING STANDARD

`dat` can also introduce a relative clause after a noun phrase.

The repeated reference to the head noun is omitted inside the relative clause when its role is clear.

Object-type relation:

```text
buk dat mi si
book that I see
```

Subject-type relation:

```text
buk dat bi gud
book that is good
```

STRING does not require separate relative words for English `who`, `which`, and `that`.

The interrogative `hu` remains the normal word for asking **who?**; it is not required merely because the relative head refers to a person.

## 6. Conditions — CONFIRMED

`hif` introduces a condition.

```text
hif + CLAUSE, CLAUSE
```

Example:

```text
hif yu go, mi wil go.
If you go, I will go.
```

Both clauses use ordinary STRING tense rules.

## 7. Hypothetical conditions — WORKING STANDARD

Use `wud` when the result is explicitly hypothetical or conditional.

```text
hif yu did go, mi wud go.
If you went, I would go.
```

STRING has no separate subjunctive verb morphology.

Context may make `wud` unnecessary; do not add it only to imitate English surface grammar.

## 8. Reasons — CONFIRMED

`bikaz` introduces a reason.

```text
CLAUSE + bikaz + CLAUSE
```

Both clauses retain normal word order.

## 9. Results — CONFIRMED

`so` joins a cause or preceding situation to its result.

```text
CLAUSE + so + CLAUSE
```

No special result verb form is created.

## 10. Clause coordination — CONFIRMED

`han`, `hor`, and `bat` can join complete clauses using the same forms used for shorter constituents.

```text
CLAUSE han CLAUSE
CLAUSE hor CLAUSE
CLAUSE bat CLAUSE
```

A repeated subject may be omitted only when doing so remains immediately clear.

## 11. Punctuation and speech — CONFIRMED

Commas help readers see clause boundaries but are not pronounced as words.

Ordinary speech may use pauses at the same boundaries.

Punctuation never repairs an otherwise ambiguous grammatical structure; word order and grammatical words carry the structure.
