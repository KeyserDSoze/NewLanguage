# 04 — Verbs, Tense, Negation, and Questions

## 1. The invariant verb — CONFIRMED

A lexical verb has one dictionary form.

It does not acquire person, tense, participle, gerund, gender, or number endings.

Core examples:

| STRING | English anchor |
|---|---|
| `bi` | be |
| `hav` | have |
| `du` | do |
| `si` | see |
| `go` | go |
| `kan` | can |
| `nid` | need |
| `lav` | love |

## 2. Present or general time — CONFIRMED

The unmarked verb expresses present, general, habitual, or contextually obvious time.

```text
mi go.
I go. / I am going in the contextually intended present sense.

mi lav yu.
I love you.
```

Aspect can be added when the speaker needs a more specific distinction.

## 3. Tense words — CONFIRMED

STRING uses three explicit tense/mood words.

| STRING | Function |
|---|---|
| `did` | past |
| `wil` | future |
| `wud` | conditional / hypothetical |

They appear before the rest of the predicate.

```text
mi did go.
I went.

mi wil go.
I will go.

mi wud go.
I would go.
```

There is no separate past form of `go`.

## 4. Progressive aspect — WORKING STANDARD

`bi` before another lexical verb marks ongoing/progressive aspect.

```text
mi bi go.
I am going.

mi did bi go.
I was going.

mi wil bi go.
I will be going.
```

The lexical verb remains unchanged.

## 5. Perfect aspect — WORKING STANDARD

`hav` before another lexical verb marks perfect aspect.

```text
mi hav go.
I have gone.

mi did hav go.
I had gone.

mi wil hav go.
I will have gone.
```

No special past participle is created.

## 6. Combining perfect and progressive — WORKING STANDARD

Perfect precedes progressive:

```text
SUBJECT + [TENSE] + HAV + BI + VERB
```

Example:

```text
mi did hav bi go.
I had been going.
```

STRING does not alter either auxiliary or the lexical verb.

## 7. Negation — CONFIRMED

The invariant negation word is `not`.

Without an explicit tense word, `not` comes before the predicate:

```text
mi not go.
I do not go.

mi not bi go.
I am not going.
```

With `did`, `wil`, or `wud`, `not` follows the tense word:

```text
mi did not go.
I did not go.

mi wil not go.
I will not go.

mi wud not go.
I would not go.
```

## 8. Modal and verb chains — CONFIRMED

A modal or dependency verb can precede the next verb directly.

```text
mi kan go.
I can go.

mi nid go.
I need to go.
```

There is no mandatory infinitive marker comparable to English `to`.

Tense remains outside the chain:

```text
mi did nid go.
I needed to go.
```

## 9. Yes/no questions — CONFIRMED

Move the first predicate word before the subject.

Present lexical verb:

```text
go yu?
Do you go? / Are you going, depending on context?
```

Copula or aspect:

```text
bi yu gud?
Are you good?
```

Past:

```text
did yu go?
Did you go?
```

Future:

```text
wil yu go?
Will you go?
```

Negative:

```text
did yu not go?
Did you not go?
```

No extra question auxiliary is inserted.

## 10. Question words — CONFIRMED

The basic question words are:

| STRING | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `wat` | what |
| `hu` | who |
| `wen` | when |
| `wer` | where |
| `wa` | why |
| `ha` | how |

A question word comes first, followed by the normal question pattern.

```text
wat si yu?
What do you see?

wer go yu?
Where do you go?

wen wil yu go?
When will you go?
```

## 11. Short answers — CONFIRMED

The basic answer words are:

```text
yes
no
```

They can stand alone.

A full answer may repeat only the information needed for clarity.
